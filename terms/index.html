Out There Together helps LGBTQ+ travellers find sailings, find each other, and plan a cruise together before they book it. A few things follow from that:

When you tap through to book a cruise, tour or activity, you're leaving Out There Together and dealing directly with the cruise line, seller or activity provider named on that page, under their own terms. We have no part in that booking, can't change or cancel it, and hold none of your payment details.

Part 2 — Community standards

The idea in one line: Out There Together exists because LGBTQ+ people don't always feel safe being themselves while travelling. Every standard below exists to protect that — for you, and for everyone else on the app.

1. Respect and harassment

Disagreement and banter are part of any social space, and we're not asking anyone to agree with everyone. What isn't welcome:

  • Hate speech, or attacking someone for who they are — their sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, disability, age, or any other protected characteristic. This applies with zero exceptions inside our own community too: biphobia, transphobia, and putting down other parts of the LGBTQ+ community are treated exactly the same as any other form of hate speech, not as banter.
  • Deliberately using the wrong name or pronouns for someone after they've told you the right ones, or deliberately revealing a former name someone has moved away from.
  • Bullying, sustained unwanted contact, pile-ons, or targeting someone across multiple chats or sailings after they've asked you to stop.
  • Threats of violence, or celebrating violence against any person or group.

2. Be who you say you are

  • Your profile should represent a real person — you. No impersonating someone else, including a celebrity, a public figure, or another member.
  • No fake or duplicate accounts. If you need a fresh start, close your old account first.
  • Use your own photos. A profile built entirely from stock or stolen images, or one that misrepresents who you are to build trust before asking for something (romantic, financial or otherwise), is a form of deception we take seriously — see section 6 on scams.

3. Protecting each other's safety and privacy

This matters more here than on a general social app, because being visibly LGBTQ+ carries real risk in some of the places our members travel through.

  • Never out anyone. Don't share, screenshot or repeat anything that reveals another member's sexual orientation, gender identity, HIV status, or anything else they've shared in confidence, outside the context they shared it in — including to other members, and especially outside the app entirely. What someone tells a sailing chat or a match is theirs to share further, not yours.
  • Don't share another member's personal details — full name, workplace, home address, phone number, or anything else that could identify or locate them offline — without their clear agreement.
  • Don't screenshot a private conversation and post or forward it without the other person's agreement.
  • If a sailing calls at a port in a country where being openly LGBTQ+ carries legal risk, the app flags this on that sailing's itinerary. Read those notices, and be thoughtful about what you post, wear, or say publicly while ashore there — for your own safety and everyone travelling with you. This is guidance, not a guarantee of safety; local law and conditions can change, and you're responsible for your own decisions ashore.

4. Nudity, sexual content and consent

Out There Together includes a feature for members to indicate romantic or sexual interest with people they choose to. That doesn't change what's acceptable in shared, visible spaces of the app:

  • Profile photos, display names and anything visible to members generally must not contain nudity or sexually explicit imagery.
  • Never send unsolicited sexual images or messages. If someone hasn't invited that conversation, don't start it — and stop the moment you're asked to.
  • Never share an intimate image of someone else without their explicit agreement to that image being shared, under any circumstances. This includes an image someone shared with you privately and trusted you to keep private.
  • No content involving minors, in any form, under any circumstances — this is reported to the relevant authorities as well as enforced on the app.
  • No soliciting or advertising commercial sexual services.

5. Meeting in person — on board and ashore

Crews, meetups and sailing chats exist to help you actually meet people, which is the whole point of Out There Together — and also the moment these standards matter most in practice.

  • Meet publicly at first, tell someone else where you're going, and trust your own judgement over anyone's persistence — this applies on board just as much as ashore.
  • A meetup listing should describe honestly what it is. Don't use a public meetup listing as a way around the standards in section 4.
  • Respect a "no". Someone declining to meet, to keep talking, or to go further than a conversation is the end of it, not a negotiation.
  • Being on the same ship doesn't reduce anyone's right to say no, to walk away, or to report a concern — including to the ship's own crew, if something happens on board that needs their attention.

6. Scams, spam and commercial activity

  • No asking other members for money, gifts, cryptocurrency or financial help — including on behalf of a "family emergency", investment opportunity, or anything else. Legitimate romantic or platonic interest never comes with a funding request.
  • No advertising, promoting a business, or recruiting for a scheme, unless you've agreed that with us first.
  • No mass messaging, chain messages, or using Out There Together to build a following or a list for use elsewhere.

7. Illegal activity and content

Don't use Out There Together to plan, promote or carry out anything illegal under the law of the country where the activity would take place, including drug dealing, human trafficking, or any activity intended to defraud someone.

8. How we enforce these standards

  • Report it. Every profile, message and meetup has a way to report a concern, and you can block or mute anyone at any time — blocking and muting don't require a report and take effect immediately for you.
  • We review every report. Depending on what we find, the outcome ranges from no action, to a warning, to removing specific content, to suspending or permanently closing the account responsible.
  • We act on severity, not just on volume. A single serious report (a safety threat, an intimate image shared without consent, content involving a minor) is treated as urgent regardless of whether anyone else has reported the same account.
  • A suspended or closed account is emailed to say so, citing a breach of these terms, conditions and community standards, without publishing the details of the report that led to it.
  • You can appeal. If you believe a decision about your account was wrong, email hello@outtheretogether.com and someone will look at it again — this isn't automated.
  • We keep a record of reports and moderation decisions so we can spot repeat behaviour, as explained in our privacy policy.
